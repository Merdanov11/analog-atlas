The algorithm

Around the crater or straight through? Whichever costs less.

  1. 01Every step has a cost: distance, climb, roughness.
  2. 02A slope that is too steep is forbidden.
  3. 03Dijkstra's algorithm finds the cheapest path on the grid.
  4. 04“String pulling” turns the zigzag into smooth arcs.

Test on a Martian plain: 1,935 km on the grid, 1,795 km after smoothing. The shortest line on the sphere: 1,795 km.

Terrain

From a 463-metre pixel to a rock at the wheel

  1. 01Original NASA elevation files are read at native resolution and smoothed bicubically to a 4 m grid.
  2. 02On top: craters from 16 to 800 m following N(>D) ~ D⁻², like real surfaces.
  3. 03Small craters, ripples and boulders are generated from coordinates: the same place always looks the same.
  4. 04The route is cut into 1.5 km stages. Any length, no seams.

Physics

A rover that feels the ground

Mars · 14 km/h · battery, power, slope, weather

Soil

Wheel sinkage after Bekker, thrust after Janosi–Hanamoto. Moon: 2–3 cm, Mars is heavier.

Suspension

1.5 t, four wheels, dampers. On the Moon it bounces: gravity is 1.62 m/s².

Energy & heat

120 kWh battery, panels depend on Sun and dust, heaters at night, radiators by day.

Weather

Dust storms: visibility drops to a few hundred metres, panel output collapses.

The drone

Two worlds, two kinds of flight

Mars: rotorcraftinspired by Ingenuity

Air 60 times thinner than Earth's: lift depends on air density. Hover takes about 4–5 kW, a flight lasts roughly 10 minutes, wind pushes it around.

Moon: rocket hopperconcept

No air, so rotors are useless. Engine thrust, 60 kg of propellant, mass dropping as it flies. Braking is limited by how far it can tilt.

The autopilot takes off, clears hills, brakes on a computed profile and lands. Autopilot, manual and fast-travel: three modes.
