How the Analog Atlas works

The question

No place on Earth is a perfect copy of the Moon or Mars, but different places match different properties. The atlas scores every land window on Earth against a chosen Moon or Mars site along three separate dimensions and lets you decide how much each one counts.

The data

  • Moon and Mars targets: NASA LRO LOLA (118 m) and MGS MOLA (463 m) elevation models, 48 km windows centred on IAU gazetteer features.
  • Earth terrain: elevation tiles built from NASA SRTM and other open sources (AWS Terrain Tiles), 48 km windows every 36 km over all land between 80 N and 80 S (about 100,000 windows), resampled to the same 480 m cell as the targets.
  • Earth climate: NASA POWER (MERRA-2) climatology: annual mean temperature, the hottest and coldest day of the year, precipitation, wind, UV index.
  • Volcanic setting: Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, distance to the nearest basaltic Holocene volcano.

The three scores (0 to 1)

  • Terrain: median and 90th-percentile slope, relief, roughness and the whole slope distribution, compared at the same 480 m scale.
  • Environment: mean temperature, annual temperature range, precipitation, air pressure (from elevation), wind and UV index. Moon and Mars values are clipped to the extremes that exist on Earth, so this dimension ranks places by how Moon- or Mars-like their climate tendency is. It never claims an exact match.
  • Ice, wind, volcanism: ground-ice potential (cold ground), wind-driven surface processes (windy and dry) and lava-flow or lava-tube setting, matched against the target's tags.

Honest limits

  • Earth has 9.8 m/s² gravity, a thick atmosphere and a protective magnetic field: it cannot reproduce lunar vacuum and gravity, Martian pressure, or radiation. These rows are always marked as different.
  • Scores are screening tools, not proof. Fine-scale geology, mineralogy and surface materials are not in the global score; use the shortlisted places as starting points for closer study.
  • Climate is a 0.5-degree reanalysis and mountain micro-climates are smoothed. Polar elevation tiles are coarser than SRTM areas. The list of well-known analog sites is ours and not exhaustive.
